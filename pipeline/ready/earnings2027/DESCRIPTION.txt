TITLE: Her Social Security Checks Stopped Coming. She Did Nothing Wrong.

Linda is 63. She claimed Social Security early, took a part-time job, and in February her check didn't arrive. Then March. Then April. Then May.

Linda isn't a real person, but every number in her story follows Social Security's real 2027 rules. If you claim before full retirement age and keep working, the retirement earnings test applies to you: earn more than $25,560 from work in 2027, and Social Security holds back $1 of benefits for every $2 over the limit, by stopping whole checks at the start of the year.

The good news: the money isn't lost. The catch: it comes back slowly.

What you'll learn:
• The 2027 earnings limit: $25,560 (up from $24,480), and $67,920 in the year you reach full retirement age
• Why Social Security stops whole checks instead of a little from each one
• What counts (wages, bonuses, self-employment profit) and what doesn't (pensions, annuities, investment income, interest, capital gains)
• How your benefit is recalculated at full retirement age, and why it can take about 14 years to earn the held checks back (SSA's own example)
• The first-year rule for people who retire mid-year ($2,130 a month in 2027)
