TITLE: Social Security Retirement Checks Are Projected to Drop 22% in Late 2032 Unless Congress Acts

There is a date in Social Security's own report: the last three months of 2032. According to the 2026 Trustees Report, that is when the retirement (OASI) trust fund's reserves are projected to run out. After that, incoming payroll taxes would cover about 78% of scheduled benefits. For retirees, that's a 22% cut, unless Congress acts.

This is a projection under current law, not a final decision. Here is what it means in dollars, why it got worse this year, what the fixes would cost, and what happened the last time Social Security came this close: 1983.

What you'll learn:
• The date: OASI reserves projected to be depleted in Q4 2032, with 78% of benefits payable (2026 Trustees Report)
• What 22% means on the average retirement check: about $477 a month in today's dollars (illustration)
• Why the retirement fund's date moved from 2033 to 2032, and why the long-term shortfall grew (per CRFB)
• The trade-off: a 34% payroll tax increase, a 25% cut to all benefits, or a 30% cut for new beneficiaries, if Congress acted today (CRFB)
• 1983: the last big fix, and how retirees and future retirees paid part of it
