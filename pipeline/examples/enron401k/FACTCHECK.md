## Claim-by-claim

### HOOK
1. **Boss called stock a bargain; 401(k) locked; thousands of workers at one of America's biggest companies.** VERIFIED. About 20,000+ plan participants (Fox/AP 2004: "more than 20,700 participants").
   - https://www.foxnews.com/story/ex-enron-workers-win-85m-in-pension-suit
2. **"by the time the lock comes off, your life savings are almost gone"**: **FIX (F1).** During the lockdown the stock fell about 28% ($13.81 to $9.98). Most of the loss came before (from about $90) and after (to under $1 by Nov 28). The current line suggests the lock caused the near-total loss. Replacement: "And within weeks, your life savings are almost gone." (lock lifted about Nov 13, bankruptcy Dec 2)

### CH1
3. **#7 on the Fortune 500 in 2001.** VERIFIED. SEC complaint vs. Skilling/Causey: "Enron rose to number seven on the Fortune 500." (2001 list, based on 2000 revenue.)
   - https://www.sec.gov/files/litigation/complaints/comp18776.pdf
4. **Stock hit about $90 in August 2000.** VERIFIED. All-time high of about $90 (90.75) on Aug 23, 2000.
   - https://mail.famous-trials.com/enron/1789-chronology ; https://www.britannica.com/event/Enron-scandal/Downfall-and-bankruptcy
5. **Match paid in Enron stock; couldn't sell the match until age 50.** VERIFIED. CNN Money: "matching contributions were always made in the form of its own stock" and employees had to hold them "until they turn 50." Sen. Lieberman's hearing statement says the same.
   - https://money.cnn.com/2001/11/26/401k/q_retire_enron_re/
   - https://www.hsgac.senate.gov/wp-content/uploads/imo/media/doc/lieberman020502.pdf
6. **">60% of 401(k) plan money in Enron stock by 2001."** VERIFIED. 62% ($1.3B) at end of 2000. Lieberman said "about two-thirds" at the peak. "By 2001" is fine.
   - https://money.cnn.com/2001/11/26/401k/q_retire_enron_re/ ; https://reason.com/2001/12/27/enrons-401k-calamity
7. **Many also bought more Enron stock with their own money.** VERIFIED (implied by the 62% share and Reason/CNN coverage. The match alone was 50% of deferrals up to 6% of pay, so most of the Enron stock came from employee money).

### CH2
8. **Skilling quit in Aug 2001 after ~6 months as CEO.** VERIFIED. He became CEO in Feb 2001 and resigned Aug 14, 2001 (SEC complaint para. 8).
9. **"The stock kept sliding."** VERIFIED (general trend Aug–Sept 2001).
10. **Sept 26, 2001 online chat; "fundamentally sound"; "incredible bargain".** VERIFIED. DOJ trial exhibit transcript of the "online chat," Sept 26, 2001, 10:00 AM CST, shows "The company is fundamentally sound." and "My personal belief is that Enron stock is an incredible bargain at current prices."
   - https://www.justice.gov/archive/enron/exhibit/03-08/BBC-0001/OCR/7644.001.TXT
11. **Stock "then around $27".** **FIX (F2).** It closed at about $25.15 on Sept 26, 2001 (Benzinga/AOL). AP says it was trading "above $25 per share that day." Plansponsor mentions "$27," but the closer figure is about $25. Use "around twenty-five dollars."
   - https://www.aol.com/company-fundamentally-sound-weeks-enron-213726005.html ; https://accesswdun.com/print/2002/1/199945 ; https://www.plansponsor.com/lay-lauds-enron-prospects-in-september-employee-meeting/
12. **A VP had already warned Lay in writing about the accounting.** VERIFIED. Sherron Watkins, VP of corporate development, sent Lay an anonymous letter on Aug 15, 2001. On Aug 22 she met him and handed over a 6-page signed letter. Both came before the Sept 26 chat.
   - https://mail.famous-trials.com/enron/1789-chronology ; https://www.britannica.com/event/Enron-scandal/Downfall-and-bankruptcy ; https://money.cnn.com/2002/01/18/companies/enron/index.htm
13. **Insiders cashed out >$1 billion of stock.** **FIX (F3), for attribution and precision.** The source is the shareholder plaintiffs' allegation (attorney Bill Lerach / Newby complaint) that 29 executives and directors "sold about $1.1 billion in stock" while results were inflated. The sales themselves are a matter of record, but the figure comes from a lawsuit and the illegality was alleged. Next to Lay's chat, the unattributed line suggests wrongdoing, so attribute it. Replacement: "And according to a shareholder lawsuit, top Enron insiders had sold about a billion dollars of stock over the previous few years."
   - https://money.cnn.com/2002/01/14/companies/enron/index.htm

### CH3
14. **Oct 16, 2001: $618M loss.** VERIFIED. Enron release: "total net loss for the quarter ... was $(618) million," including $1.01B in after-tax charges.
   - https://picker.uchicago.edu/Enron/EarningsRelease%2810-16-01%29.pdf
15. **"The stock fell hard."** **FIX (F4).** The big drop came over the following days (SEC inquiry news Oct 22, CFO ousted Oct 24), not on Oct 16 itself. Replacement: "In the days that followed, the stock fell hard."
16. **"And right then, the 401(k) plan was frozen."** **FIX (F5).** By Enron's account the blackout began Oct 26 (first business day affected: Oct 29), ten days after the loss announcement. The plaintiffs alleged Oct 17. Saying "right then" asserts the workers' version as fact. Replacement: "And days later, the 401(k) plan was frozen." The script's next sentences already handle the dispute.
17. **Enron said it was a long-planned switch to a new administrator.** VERIFIED. Hewitt was chosen as successor recordkeeper in May 2001, replacing Northern Trust Retirement Consulting. Northern Trust was also trustee, succeeded by Wilmington Trust.
   - https://www.plansponsor.com/a-change-in-plans-the-enron-conversion/
18. **Workers could not sell, not a single share.** VERIFIED for plan accounts during the blackout (no fund transfers allowed).
19. **Enron's dates Oct 26–Nov 13; $13.81 to $9.98; workers say earlier.** VERIFIED, with a note. Enron's own descriptions vary: Oct 26–Nov 12 (Reason, 12 trading days), Oct 29–Nov 13 (Lieberman citing Enron), and Oct 26–Nov 19 (Enron spokeswoman to CNN, for the full transfer restriction). The accelerated go-live was Nov 13. "$13.81 on Oct 26 to $9.98" is in Reason and Plansponsor. The suit alleged Oct 17; a union official testified that employees were locked out from mid-September or Oct 19. Acceptable as written. A safer optional version: "By Enron's account, the freeze ran from late October to mid-November."
   - https://reason.com/2001/12/27/enrons-401k-calamity ; https://www.plansponsor.com/company-enron-participants-only-blacked-out-for-10-days/ ; https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/CHRG-107shrg78616/html/CHRG-107shrg78616.htm
20. **Bankruptcy Dec 2, 2001; stock below $1.** VERIFIED, with **FIX (F6)** for sequence. The stock was already at $0.61 on Nov 28 after Dynegy walked away. Replacement: "By then, the stock was below one dollar."
21. **Worker $472,000 to about $40,000 (Roy Rinard).** **FIX (F7).** CNN: Rinard's account fell "from $470,000 a year ago to around $40,000." Union testimony at the Senate (Feb 5, 2002) lists "Roy Rinard ... $472,000 loss," which describes the loss, not the starting balance. The two figures don't fit together exactly, so round: "about four hundred seventy thousand ... about forty thousand."
   - https://money.cnn.com/2001/11/26/401k/q_retire_enron_re/ ; https://www.hsgac.senate.gov/wp-content/uploads/imo/media/doc/miller1.pdf
22. **Retiree says he lost $1.3M (Charles Prestwood).** VERIFIED. Senate Commerce subcommittee testimony, Dec 18, 2001: he "has lost $1.3 million." (His holdings were largely ESOP/Enron shares. "Says he lost" is the right framing.)
   - https://thinkadvisor.com/2001/12/19/enron-retirement-plan-members-testify-on-capitol-hill
23. **Workers lost >$1 billion in retirement plans.** **FIX (F8), attribution.** This is the plaintiffs' claim: the suit alleged "they lost more than $1 billion." It is plausible, given $1.3B in Enron stock in the 401(k) at end of 2000 plus ESOP holdings. Replacement: "In total, workers say they lost more than a billion dollars in their retirement plans." The closing line "paid a billion dollars to learn" is fine as rhetoric.
   - https://www.foxnews.com/story/ex-enron-workers-win-85m-in-pension-suit

### CH4
24. **"Settlements paid back tens of millions."** **FIX (F9).** This understates the recovery. The Tittle v. Enron recoveries include $85M (insurance, 2004), $37.5M (Northern Trust, 2006), Andersen ($25–40M), a Skilling victims' fund, a $12M claim on the Lay estate, and a $356M allowed bankruptcy claim against Enron. Cash alone is well over $100M. Replacement: "Years later, settlements paid back part of it." The rest of the line ("A fraction of what was lost") stays.
   - https://www.hbsslaw.com/cases/enron
25. **$85M insurance deal.** VERIFIED. May 2004 partial settlement funded by an "$85 million insurance policy" covering the plan fiduciaries.
26. **$37.5M from the plan's trustee.** VERIFIED. Northern Trust, 2006, paid entirely by its insurers. Northern Trust was the plan trustee and prior recordkeeper (Plansponsor conversion article). Optional: "from the plan's former trustee."
   - https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0000073124/000119312506070098/dex99.htm ; https://www.plansponsor.com/ntc-settles-enron-company-stock-case/
27. **SOX blackout notice "since 2002", "about thirty days".** **FIX (F10).** Sarbanes-Oxley §306(b) was enacted July 30, 2002, but the notice requirement took effect for blackouts on or after **Jan 26, 2003**. Notice must come at least 30 (and no more than 60) days in advance, with exceptions for unforeseeable events. It applies to individual-account plans such as 401(k)s. Replacement: "Since 2003, your plan generally has to warn you at least thirty days before a 401(k) blackout."
   - https://www.dol.gov/newsroom/releases/ebsa/ebsa20030123 ; https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/FR-2003-01-24/pdf/03-1431.pdf
28. **PPA: right to sell company stock "in most 401(k)s", including the match after 3 years, "since 2006".** **FIX (F11).** The PPA was signed Aug 2006 but took effect for plan years beginning after Dec 31, 2006 (so 2007). It covers only defined contribution plans that hold **publicly traded** employer securities, with exceptions for certain stand-alone ESOPs. Most 401(k)s don't hold employer stock at all, so "most 401(k)s" is wrong. Employee contributions can be diversified immediately. Employer contributions can be restricted until 3 years of service. At least 3 diversified alternative options must be offered. Replacement: "And since 2007, if your 401(k) holds your employer's publicly traded stock, you have the right to sell it. Including the company match, after three years on the job." (IRC 401(a)(35) / ERISA 204(j).)
   - https://thinkadvisor.com/tax-facts/2024/03/13/3732-what-is-the-diversification-requirement-for-defined-contribution-plans ; https://www.stayexempt.irs.gov/pub/irs-drop/n-06-107.pdf

### CH5
29. **"The rules can warn you. They can't force you to sell."** VERIFIED. Both rules give notice or a right; neither requires anyone to diversify.
30. **"Many workers still hold a big piece of their retirement in their own employer's stock."** **FIX (F12), overstated.** Vanguard (2024 report, 2023 data): 8% of plans offer company stock (down from 11% in 2005); 7% of all participants hold it (24% in 2005); only 2% have more than 20% of their account in it (14% in 2005). Concentration persists mostly at large public companies. Replacement: "Some workers, especially at big public companies, still hold a big piece of their retirement in their own employer's stock." Optionally add the stat: "Vanguard says about one in fifty of its 401(k) savers has more than a fifth of their account in company stock."
   - https://workplace.vanguard.com/insights-and-research/report/taking-stock-of-company-stock-and-its-risks-in-a-401k-plan.html
31. **"This is not financial advice."** OK.

## Legal-risk notes
- The script does not accuse Lay of a crime, which is good. If any later edit mentions his conviction, say: "Lay was convicted of fraud and conspiracy in 2006, but he died before sentencing, and under the law his conviction was wiped out (vacated)." Judge Simeon Lake vacated it in Oct 2006 under the abatement doctrine.
  - https://www.jurist.org/news/2006/10/federal-judge-vacates-lay-conviction/
- Putting the Watkins warning, the "incredible bargain" chat, and the $1B insider sales side by side suggests deliberate deception. Each fact is accurate, but attributing the insider-sales figure to the lawsuit (F3) lowers the risk. Don't add "lied" or "insider trading" for Lay.
- "Workers say it started even earlier" and "workers say they lost..." (F8) correctly frame the plaintiffs' allegations as allegations.
- Skilling's convictions stood (sentence later reduced), but he isn't accused of anything in the script.
